## Hook (the counterintuitive claim, in one line)
Your ego will lose control of your life either way — the only question is whether you hand it over now, on purpose, to the thing you actually want to do, or have it stripped from you at the end when it's too late to matter.

## Who this is for (the specific, often-overlooked person)
The person avoiding their real calling because of ego — fear of looking dumb, of others' opinions, of failing publicly, of not being immediately good. They're protecting an ego that time is going to dismantle regardless.

## The argument
- The ego resists the leap (starting the thing, posting badly, being a beginner in public) to protect its self-image.
- But the ego doesn't win long-term — it "surrenders itself anyway" (age, death, reality) or you die still clinging to it. Either way it loses. So the resistance buys nothing.
- **The turn:** given that the surrender is coming no matter what, the *only* variable you control is whether you surrender it *toward something you want* — now, while it counts. Voluntary ego-death → your calling.
- Ancient version (his niche's ancient→modern move): every mystical tradition says *die before you die* — ego-dissolution (fana, "the Belly of the Whale") is the precondition for rebirth ([[sources/perennial-philosophy-notes]]). Creating anything new requires the old you to die first.
