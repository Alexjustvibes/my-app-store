## Hook (the counterintuitive claim, in one line)
You were told your scattered interests were a character flaw to fix. They were the one asset the AI age actually rewards — you just never learned to point them at a single thing.

## Who this is for (the specific, often-overlooked person)
The multi-interested person who feels *behind* — the one who's been called flaky, told to "focus," who watches specialists get promoted while they can't stay in one lane. The fellow ENTP ([[my-reader-profile-entp]]) whose Problem #9 is "too many interests / can't commit" ([[problems-log]]). They've spent years apologizing for the exact thing that's now the edge. This is the human writing to his past self.

## The argument
- The "pick a lane" doctrine is ~250 years old — an *industrial* invention, not a law of nature. Before the factory, the free person ran a whole operation (artisan, farmer, Da Vinci); the single-task specialist was the *slave*. "In The Renaissance, nobody gave a shit about your niche. They gave a shit about what you were capable of." ([[sources/koe-digital-renaissance-polymaths]], [[concepts/polymathy]])
- AI automates the *specialist's* single task first. The generalist who can run the whole operation — content, product, judgment — is the one it accelerates, not replaces. The safe-looking lane was the exposed one.
- **The turn / what most people get wrong:** breadth alone isn't the win — that's just "dabbling," and it's why you felt scattered. The move isn't *fewer* interests; it's **one vessel** they all pour into. "Do it all, but channel it into one craft." The flaw was never the interests. It was the missing funnel. (This is the reframe of Problem #9: not "commit to less," but "give the breadth a single mouth to speak through.")
- Proof it compounds: your uniqueness *is* the combination — the jacked philosopher beats the armchair philosopher. Nobody else has your exact stack, so the intersection can't be copied ([[concepts/specific-knowledge]], [[seeds/your-free-obsession-is-the-moat]]).
