## Hook (the counterintuitive claim, in one line)
Feeling like you have nothing to offer isn't a reason to wait — it's the starting instruction. The first thing you "provide" is the solution to a problem you had yourself.

## Who this is for (the specific, often-overlooked person)
The aspiring creator/builder frozen by "who am I to teach anything / I have no expertise." They're waiting to become valuable before they start, when the path to value runs *through* their own problems.

## The argument
- You don't need pre-existing expertise or authority. You need *a problem you've solved.* Everyone has those.
- Solve your own problem → the solution is your first offering → the story of solving it is your first content ([[problems-log]] — his whole system: your problems = your products).
- "Free yourself, then free others" (Koe): self-improvement is the raw material of other-improvement.
- The turn: the people who feel they have "nothing to offer" usually have the most to offer — they just haven't reframed their solved problems as value. Scarcity of *credentials* ≠ scarcity of *value*.
