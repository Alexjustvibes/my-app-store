# Jerking Awake from the Hypnosis of High Achievement — Harry Miller

> Source: Harry Miller (Substack), 2025-05-23. Subtitle: "Thoughts of an American golden boy who can't even find a job." Clipped by the human 2026-06-09.
> Faithful preservation, dialogue lightly condensed. Immutable.

## The interview (Crunch Fitness)
A satirical scene: the narrator — valedictorian, 4.0 in Mechanical Engineering, Rhodes Finalist, invited to the White House, on the Today Show, Ohio State football — is interviewed by "Linda," a manager who can barely register his résumé ("A Rhodes Finalist… what's that?"). She asks, "In fact, why are you here?" He answers: "This is the only place that called back." She hires him: part-time, minimum wage, opening at 4 AM, stocking drinks "labels facing out," trained by Mahmoud, a high-school junior. "If you keep at it!…"

## The city
Staring up at the Empire State Building ("the might and enterprise of 20th-century America") then at 1 Seaport — a 60-story skeleton halted in 2020 because it was leaning. "Maybe that's why I can't find a job. This town is terrified of engineers now." Amazon and DoorDash workers doing a "capitalist scavenger hunt": click check, scan QR, next, "Would you like to leave a tip?"

## The firehouse
He stops by in person to ask for work. Endless loop: "the online application is closed" / "did you apply online?" / "try again in 5 years." "How did you make it?" — "Somebody gave me a break a long time ago." "Can I have one?" — "Nope. We're all out."

## The apartment / the feed
Unemployed, pan by the bed to catch roof leak. LinkedIn "flashbangs" him. Grift content ("6 months ago I was broke, now I'm eating caviar with models… 10 tips"). 10 unanswered DMs to alumni. A "perfect match" job: entry-level but demands 3 years' experience, $10 above minimum, "fast paced," "just like family." Wants a cover letter. He writes one he doesn't believe ("always been passionate about working in a beige office… driven by my deep desire for conformity and dying inside"), runs it through an AI. Considers hiding that he's white and a man; debates claiming disability ("technically I tried to kill myself in college… I'll do anything to give myself an edge"). Knows "some AI bot named Linda" will see 4 of 5 keywords and trash it without a reply.

## The lament (the essay's argument)
- "I was a promising kid once… I did everything that I was supposed to… I played by the rules, and I got played. I needed just one break, and for that, I got broken."
- The broken inheritance: he expected that "two decades of servile obedience, prefect grades, and a gung ho attitude" would earn a living wage, a family, maybe an above-ground pool. "It doesn't even have to be in-ground! I'm not that ambitious!"
- Not nostalgia — "generationally jealous." Cleaning up a mess he didn't make. Corporations buying the housing market; middlemen everywhere, "half the time not even human." "The food is poison. And the poison is delicious. Our hobbies are surrogates for meaning… avoidant strategies." "What used to be divinely inspired is now crowd-sourced and ordained by group-think."
- **Powerlessness & autonomy:** "Why do I have to 'ask' apps not to track me across my device? I'm the fucking Homo sapien here. Aren't I in charge?… Do I have any power left?" "The modern man lives longer than any of his predecessors, yet he has the least freedom of them all." Needs a permit to build anything, authorization to work the land, redundant at every step — "just another nameless, gerrymandered statistic."
- **Waking up:** "We are all waking up from the hypnosis… We see all the goal posts that keep moving. We're not stupid kids anymore… you can't just roll out Bill Nye and expect me to trust the experts."
- "I want autonomy, sovereignty, dignity, and to believe in my efficacy. But the only thing I have learned so far from the grown ups is helplessness."
- **The power-process / weakness diagnosis:** "The feeling of 'having power'… is cultivated by the effortful attainment of goals… seeing that one's actions have influence on their environment in a controllable way." The functionally weak "exist without effect— inconsequentially— and this proves the most profound psychological wound." In its absence, the disenfranchised "seek powerful groups… to belong to" and the highest honor becomes being "a zealous rule follower. A loyal worker. A protocol droid." But the goals achieved are "rehashed, redundant, trivial… busy work… Lifetime achievements in a worthless, virtual game." (To earn his degree he wrote "lab reports for experiments first written in 1997.")
- **Attention:** "We are so adequately invested into the dopamine haze of our broadcasted distraction. (Attention is a thing that you pay with, too, you know.)"
- "We are prepared to debilitate ourselves over real work." The disillusioned high-achievers — "the punks, the jocks… even the honor roll students" — are "waking up now." Sick of "pizza parties with thimblefuls of orange soda."
- Close: the "old growth forest" of mainstream society has shaded the saplings too long, but the deadfall is dry kindling "and all they need now is a spark." "A break is coming. One way or another." — "(For clarity's sake, this is not a call to violence. Cheers!)"
