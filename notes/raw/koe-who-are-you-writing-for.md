# Who Are You Writing For? — Dan Koe

> Source: Dan Koe (course module). Continues the Domain of Mastery material; introduces Social Leverage and the 2 Hour Content Ecosystem. Clipped by the human 2026-06-09. Immutable.

Let's make this as simple as possible. You are writing to your past, present, and future self.

That is who you have the most experience with. That is what attracts the people you can help the most. That delivers the most value to people 1-3 steps behind you. Whether beginner or advanced, this is the easiest way to avoid saturation, be unique, attract a tribe, and help the community you can help most.

This is not a course on branding or social media growth. It's a course on writing and building a content ecosystem, assuming you write online to build a personal brand around what you love.

## Your 3rd Person Public Journal
I view all my online writing as a public journal. 90% of my tweets are tips, tricks, realizations, and advice to a version of myself.
- Past self: advice on traps to avoid.
- Present self: what I should be doing to see success.
- Future self: motivation/inspiration from the lens of a self that already accomplished those things.

The secret: write as if speaking to another person, or to yourself in 3rd person.

In traditional branding, people create a customer avatar to understand who they create content/products/marketing for. Templates ask age, interests, demographics, where they hang out, favorite quotes. **The most important aspect of a buyer persona is a list of problems that keep them up at night.**

For personal branding, **your customer avatar is you. The problems you solve with your content are your problems.** By cultivating self-awareness you have all the marketing firepower you need (if you aren't overthinking). With a personal brand you attract people who vibe with your message, interests, and expertise (while persuading them those things matter to their quality of life). A strong personal brand is the best USP.

(If you're selling a specific service to a specific person — e.g. web design to local businesses — yes, make a separate avatar. But that's not the way to build an audience with the flexibility to write whatever you want and enjoy your work for life.)

There's a website that generates your customer avatar and helps you understand yourself: **take the MBTI personality test.** It gives a deeper understanding of yourself, makes you aware of your problems, and gives ample firepower for who you're writing for.

### Attract the people you can help the most
People with similar interests and goals; you expose them to related interests/goals. Your content (and products) should target this specific person's problems and aspirations.

## The Key To Building A Leverageable Brand
Think of your writing on a longer time scale. One tweet/newsletter doesn't display the depth behind your writing. Neither does a week of tweets or a month of newsletters. **Your content over a 6-12 month timeframe determines your followers' perception of you.** It's rare to go from non-follower to die-hard fan in under a month.

During those 6-12 months, touch on 3 pillars of **Social Leverage.** High social leverage (all 3 bases) opens more opportunities — a brand with high social leverage is noticeable. (Koe: I can DM almost anyone and be taken seriously, close more clients, get on big podcasts by asking, exchange cross-platform value. Build it on one platform, transfer it 10x easier — reach out, say you have X followers and useful strategies, they'll help you grow. Plus validated content ideas transfer over.)

An account focused on only one pillar (quotes/theme/meme/super-niche-skill account) won't have these opportunities — ignored in DMs, struggle to earn. (Caveat: super-niche accounts can make money, but do they enjoy the process? Can they talk about what gives them energy, or stick to a script?)

The 3 pillars to solidify over 6-12 months: **growth, authority, authenticity.**

### 1) Growth
If your brand isn't growing (followers/subscribers), something must change. There's always something you can do to grow faster (you may lack the specific knowledge yet). Growth is determined by content + top-of-funnel socials (Twitter, Instagram, LinkedIn). Is your content being shared? Will it make people click your profile and wonder "Who is this guy?" Is it polarizing due to speaking with confidence? If not growing, play to the algorithm a bit more — study what's working, incorporate it, get it shared by connections.

### 2) Authority
Authority = depth + social proof.
- **Depth** = deep understanding of one area where you create unique perspectives and compelling arguments for specific problems. Comes from long-form content (newsletters, blogs). If you're on Twitter but not writing newsletters, you're shooting yourself in the foot.
- **Social proof** = credentials, titles, niche accomplishments, testimonials. When starting out you won't have much authority — that's fine. Establish it fast with long-form content and actionable advice.

### 3) Authenticity
Authenticity comes from:
- Documenting projects you're working on. Everything is a project — mental, physical, spiritual, financial development can all be spun into lessons for those 2-3 steps behind.
- Expressing opinions, likes, dislikes to relate with your audience.
- Being vulnerable — but not pitifully. Be vulnerable by talking about your losses and what you'll do differently.

### All of these intersect
If just starting / no authority yet, focus there first — a mix of growth and authority is great for beginners. Once authority is established you can pivot (the content you made to build it stays up forever on your blog).
