# What Do You Write About? — Dan Koe

> Source: Dan Koe (course module, the "Domain of Mastery" material; references the 2 Hour Content Ecosystem). Clipped by the human 2026-06-09. Immutable.

Writing is more than just a tool to attract attention. As you will come to find, writing is the key to clear thinking, true learning, and deep understanding.

Writing is how you "connect the dots." The dots being your thoughts, ideas, and the purpose behind them.

For that reason, it only makes sense to treat your writing as a vessel for self-mastery, skill-mastery, and interest-mastery.

With that, we need to find a balance of interests that:
- You are able to monetize in the future (so we can write full time)
- You are curious to learn more about (so you are excited to learn and write every day)
- Allows you to understand yourself and the world for maximum impact of your writing

When the balance of all of these are met, writing becomes seamless and life becomes fun.

## Your Money-Making Interest
In reality, this interest can be anything. It should be something you can see yourself doing for a long time.

If you are unsure, understand the **eternal markets** and what people are already buying. The eternal markets are **health, wealth, relationships, and happiness.** That's where burning problems are; everyone you follow on social media is selling products that target problems in the eternal markets.

These break down into **sub-markets** like fitness, online business, dating, and spirituality. (Best markets to build an audience around and write about.)

Those break down further into **niche-markets** like bodybuilding, content creation, game, and mindfulness meditation. (Best markets to target a burning problem and sell a product/service around.)

## Your Genuine Interest
What are you excited to learn more about?
- The things in your browser/YouTube history
- The books you buy or want to buy
- The interest you've been wanting to explore but putting off

For me, this is spirituality and consciousness. I can't help you much with this — I am not you. What could you see yourself learning about for a long time? What is the main thing YOU want to write about?

Pick something. Be decisive. Start writing. You can change it at any time. Second-guessing yourself will only delay you finding your true interest or passion.

## Your Developmental Interest
You evolve. Your writing evolves. Others evolve. If you want to bring true value (impact people, help them grow), study development: psychology, spirituality, metaphysics, self-improvement, philosophy — anything that helps you understand yourself.

If you understand yourself, you understand others (from a broad lens). If you understand others, your writing improves — you get better at delivering value and generating opportunity.

## Breaking Down Your Interests For Easy Writing
Example broad interest — online business. Break it into:

**1) The principles, basics, or fundamentals.** A game isn't fun if you don't know the rules. Writing these down gives you a foundation for an evergreen newsletter/video/article/lead magnet to send people to when they ask "How do I do X?" For online business: create a product/service; learn marketing & sales to communicate its value; send traffic (social, paid ads, referrals). How do YOU get creative with the fundamentals?

**2) Topics & sub-topics to expand awareness of the space.** Dive into the iceberg of topics. Attacking your interest from all angles creates depth and authority. Each becomes short-form and a series of long-form ideas: self-employment, productivity, focused work, creating your niche, client acquisition, how to DM, qualifying, service onboarding, social media growth, writing newsletters, writing threads, best types of content, digital product creation, marketing and offers, launch strategy, best platforms. Notice sub-topics branch into domains outside online business — if you break things down enough, everything connects. People will know your expertise from your other content.

**3) Mentors to emulate from those topics.** Follow people who've put in the time and have results. (He uses TweetHunter and Twemex to write/schedule/generate ideas from writers he aspires to.) This is interest immersion — follow everyone in the subject, buy top books, expose yourself left and right — you become a member of that niche with market research at your fingertips.

**4) Content ideas that are validated and nearly guarantee engagement.** From your topic breakdown + mentors, ideas flood in.

**5) Real-world problems for compelling content angles.** Immersed in the environment, you identify others' problems and your own. Create content around these or note them down for when you run out of ideas.

## Broadening Your Interests
Common questions: "What interests should I choose? What if mine are too niche? What if people aren't interested?"

If you are interested in something, other people are. You aren't that special ;)

If your interest feels too niche, broaden it — move up a market stage (niche → sub → eternal). Instead of "email marketing," make it "online business" / "freelancing" / "marketing." We're aiming to build a large audience; don't box yourself in too soon. You can still primarily talk about email marketing, but you'll get more readers when using email marketing TO explain marketing concepts. Bodybuilding → health/fitness. Meditation → spirituality/mindset.

Once you have your 3 interests to master, you can write about anything related to them.

## Ideas For Your First Topic
Before idea generation, flesh out the **"why"** behind your Domain of Mastery. It builds authority and makes you think about why others should care. The "why" is your greatest tool in all content — it sets you apart and displays your unique perspective/philosophy.
